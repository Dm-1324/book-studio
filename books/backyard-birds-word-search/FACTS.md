# Fact check: Backyard Birds Word Search for Seniors  (2026-11-20)

**FACTS CHANGED: 27 of 60 facts were reworded in puzzles.yaml. The book MUST BE REBUILT (interior.pdf, answers, and anything that prints the facts) before upload.**

Method: every row below was checked by opening (WebFetch) the listed page(s). Cornell Lab (allaboutbirds.org) and Britannica returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so they were NOT used as sources. A fact that an opened page did not support at the same strength was reworded to what the page does say (status VERIFIED applies to the new wording).

Other factual text: book.yaml `how_to_play` / `back_blurb` and listing.md make no bird claims (only product claims: 60 puzzles, left-to-right/top-to-bottom, answers at the back), so nothing to verify there.

## Changed facts (old -> new)

1. "Chickadees hide seeds and remember where they put them." -> "Chickadees store seeds and other food to eat later." (Audubon says they store food to recover later; no page opened states they remember where.)
2. "Mockingbirds can copy the songs of dozens of other birds." -> "Mockingbirds often imitate the songs of other birds." (no number of species found.)
3. "A diving peregrine falcon is the fastest animal on Earth." -> "The peregrine falcon is the world's fastest bird, diving at over 100 miles per hour." (sources open say fastest bird, not fastest animal.)
4. "Owls can't move their eyes, so they turn their whole head." -> "Owls' eyes are fixed in their sockets, so they turn their whole head." (source says fixed sockets, can't rotate.)
5. "Ducks waterproof their feathers with oil from a gland by the tail." -> "Birds use oil from a special gland to waterproof their feathers." (opened source says birds, gland location/ducks not stated.)
6. "Birds sing most at first light: this is called the dawn chorus." -> "Many birds sing loudest around sunrise: this is called the dawn chorus." (RSPB: peaks just before and after sunrise.)
7. "Binoculars marked 8x42 make birds look 8 times closer." -> "Binoculars marked 8x42 magnify birds eight times." (Audubon wording.)
8. "Arctic terns fly from the Arctic to the Antarctic and back each year." -> "Arctic terns migrate from the Arctic to the Antarctic every year." (return leg not stated explicitly.)
9. "A puffin can carry ten or more small fish in its beak at once." -> "A puffin may carry a dozen small fish in its bill at once." (Audubon says a dozen.)
10. "Woodpeckers have long tongues that wrap around the inside of their skull." -> "Woodpecker tongues are so long they wrap around the skull when not in use." (NPS wording; 'inside' not supported.)
11. "Crows can recognize and remember individual human faces." -> "Crows can recognize individual people." (Audubon states identification; memory not stated on the opened page.)
12. "Penguins cannot fly, but they are superb swimmers." -> "Penguins cannot fly, but emperor penguins can dive deeper than any other bird." (NatGeo emperor penguin page.)
13. "A roadrunner can run at around 20 miles per hour." -> "A roadrunner can run about 15 miles per hour." (Audubon says 15 mph; 20 mph not found.)
14. "In spring, many male songbirds sing to claim a territory and attract a mate." -> "In spring, many songbirds sing to attract a mate and defend a territory." (NatGeo: both genders sing to find a mate; males also defend territory.)
15. "Birds have no teeth, so many swallow small stones to help grind their food." -> "Some birds swallow small stones or grit to help grind their food." ('no teeth' not confirmed on an opened page.)
16. "A shallow birdbath gives birds water to drink and bathe in all year." -> "A birdbath gives birds fresh water, which they especially need in winter." (Audubon: birds especially need open water in winter.)
17. "A bird's beak is bone covered with keratin, like our fingernails." -> "The outer part of a bird's beak is made of keratin, like our fingernails." (Audubon wording.)
18. "Blue feathers get their color from tiny structures that scatter light, not blue pigment." -> "Blue feathers get their color from tiny structures that scatter light." ('not pigment' not stated explicitly.)
19. "Nightjars hunt moths and other insects at dusk and at night." -> "Nightjars hunt moths and other insects at night." (opened page does not state dusk feeding.)
20. "Many shorebirds probe the mud with long bills to find worms and shellfish." -> "Many shorebirds probe the mud with their bills to find worms and other prey." (opened page says worms and other prey; shellfish/long not stated.)
21. "Weaver birds knot strands of grass into hanging nests." -> "Male weaver birds build hanging nests from strips of leaves." (grass knots not found on opened pages.)
22. "Some swiftlets build their nests almost entirely from their own saliva." -> "Some swiftlets build their nests from their own saliva." (source says edible nests are made of swiftlet saliva; 'almost entirely' dropped.)
23. "Early morning is often the best time to see and hear birds." -> "Many birds sing more at dawn and dusk, so those are great times to listen." (NPS wording; 'best time' not stated.)
24. "A list of all the birds you have ever seen is called a life list." -> "A life list is a birder's record of every species they have seen." (NPS wording.)
25. "Bird photographers often use long lenses so they can stay far away." -> "Bird photographers use long lenses to keep a respectful distance." (Audubon ethical guide.)
26. "Eagles can spot small prey from very far away." -> "Golden eagles can spot a rabbit from over a mile away." (Audubon, golden eagle.)
27. "The dawn chorus is often at its loudest in spring." -> "The dawn chorus is at its loudest in spring." (RSPB: season mid-March to mid-June, peak early May.)
